## Conclusie voor de eigenaar (NL)

1. Faceless is de norm: zeven van de acht bureaus tonen geen oprichter. Onze keuze is geen handicap.
2. De sterke boutiques winnen niet op volume maar op drie dingen die wij nog missen: een transparant fee- en garantieblok op de werkgeverspagina, stad x vakgebied landingspagina's, en zichtbaar bewijs (cases, gevulde vacatures in een archief).
3. Op ontwerp missen wij vier feiten die concurrenten wel tonen: een live vacaturecount met zoekveld in de hero, het salaris op kaart en detailpagina, een plakkende sollicitatiebalk op mobiel, en de garantie herhaald naast elke call-to-action. Alles past in het bestaande ontwerpsysteem; geen foto's nodig.
4. Vacatures: 27 in vier niches is boutique-schaal en op zich verdedigbaar. Verbreed selectief naar drie aanpalende disciplines (cloud/IoT voor embedded, functional safety, systems engineering) en groei naar 40 tot 45 vacatures uit echte klantopdrachten. Niet naar generalist-volume.
5. Diensten: eerst de goedkope zaken die geen juridische toets nodig hebben (landingspagina's, salarisgids uit openbare bronnen, casebeschrijvingen, archief van gevulde rollen). Het fee- en garantieblok wacht op de commerciële voorwaarden van de eigenaar.

## Summary for the owner (EN)

1. Faceless is the market norm: seven of eight agencies show no founder.
2. The strong boutiques win on a transparent fee and guarantee block, city x discipline landing pages, and visible proof (cases, an archive of filled roles), not on volume.
3. On design we lack four facts competitors show: a live vacancy count with a search box in the hero, salary on card and detail page, a sticky apply bar on mobile, and the guarantee repeated next to every call to action. All fit the existing design system without photos.
4. Vacancies: 27 in four niches is boutique scale. Widen selectively into cloud/IoT for embedded, functional safety and systems engineering, and grow to 40 to 45 live vacancies from real client orders.
5. Services: build the cheap items without legal review first; the fee and guarantee block waits for the owner's commercial terms.

### Samenvatting (NL)

We bekeken acht bureaus die kandidaten en klanten in Brainport ook tegenkomen: Computer Futures (SThree), De ICT Recruiter, Iduet, TMC, Brunel, ALTEN, YER en iSense. De sterkste sites combineren vier dingen: een helder, direct proces met concrete termijnen (48 uur, 14-21 dagen, 5 werkdagen), een transparant fee-model zonder verrassingen (De ICT Recruiter: vast percentage, no cure no pay, garantietermijn), stadsspecifieke of vakgebied-specifieke landingpagina's voor SEO, en zichtbare social proof (klantlogo's, cases met concrete uitkomst). Vrijwel niemand toont een oprichter; het faceless-model is standaard in deze markt, wat aansluit bij GSP's eigen keuze. Generalisten (Brunel, ALTEN, TMC, YER) draaien op volume: honderden vacatures, brede disciplines, weinig salarisinfo. Boutique-bureaus (ICT Recruiter, Iduet) draaien op focus: 10-30 functiecategorieën, senior/executive nadruk, transparante fee, sterke kennisbank.

Onze 27 vacatures in vier disciplines (embedded 8, mechatronica 7, OT-security 6, test 6) zijn qua omvang vergelijkbaar met een kleine boutique, maar smaller dan de vier generalisten die honderden posities per regio draaien. Verbreding naar aanpalende disciplines (cloud/IoT voor embedded, functional safety, systems engineering, industrial IT) is aan te raden, mits het via echte klantopdrachten gebeurt en niet via schaduw-vacatures. Op diensten loopt GSP achter op salarisgidsen, klant-cases en stadslandingpagina's; dat zijn de goedkoopste en effectiefste dingen om eerst te bouwen.

### Summary (EN)

We reviewed eight agencies that Brainport embedded/mechatronics/OT clients and candidates also encounter: Computer Futures (SThree), De ICT Recruiter, Iduet, TMC, Brunel, ALTEN, YER, and iSense. The strongest sites combine four things: a clear, direct process with concrete turnaround claims (48 hours, 14-21 days, 5 working days), a transparent fee model with no surprises (De ICT Recruiter: flat percentage, no cure no pay, replacement guarantee), city- or discipline-specific landing pages for SEO, and visible social proof (client logos, cases with concrete outcomes). Almost none show a named founder; the faceless model is standard in this market, matching GSP's own choice. Generalists (Brunel, ALTEN, TMC, YER) run on volume: hundreds of vacancies, broad disciplines, little salary transparency. Boutique firms (ICT Recruiter, Iduet) run on focus: 10-30 role categories, senior/executive emphasis, transparent fee, strong knowledge-base content.

Our 27 vacancies across four disciplines (embedded 8, mechatronics 7, OT-security 6, test 6) are comparable in scale to a small boutique, but narrower than the four generalists running hundreds of positions per region. Widening into adjacent disciplines (cloud/IoT for embedded, functional safety, systems engineering, industrial IT) is worth pursuing, provided it comes from real client orders rather than filler listings. On services, GSP lags on salary guides, client case studies, and city landing pages; those are the cheapest and most effective things to build first.

### What makes them good: recurring patterns

1. **Concrete, falsifiable speed or process claims, stated once and specifically, not vague urgency.** Computer Futures: 48 hours to candidate profiles. Iduet: 14-21 days to first interviews. Iduet testimonial: 5 werkdagen. De ICT Recruiter deliberately refuses a hard number and states why. Pattern: the strong sites either give one specific, checkable number or explicitly explain why they won't. None stack multiple vague claims. GSP's honesty rule (no fabricated stats/urgency) fits the De ICT Recruiter approach best: state process steps plainly, only add a number once it is true and can be defended.

2. **A single transparent fee/guarantee structure, stated in plain language.** De ICT Recruiter: flat 20% of gross annual salary incl. holiday pay, no cure no pay, no start-up fee, two-month replacement guarantee, no exclusivity required. This is the clearest, most NRC/FD-register example among the eight, direct, no hedging, no "neem contact op voor een offerte." Worth adapting almost verbatim in tone for GSP's client-facing pricing copy, once GSP's actual terms are confirmed internally.

3. **Faceless-but-not-anonymous branding is the market norm, not an outlier.** 7 of 8 firms show no named founder (only De ICT Recruiter does, and it is a genuine one-person boutique, not a brand choice). This validates GSP's own "wij not ik" rule, it is not out of step with the market, it is the market.

4. **City x discipline landing-page matrices for SEO.** Iduet: 7 city pages with near-identical service copy per city (Arnhem, Den Haag, Utrecht, Rotterdam, Eindhoven, Amsterdam, Schiphol). iSense: parallel "per regio" and "per vakgebied" listing pages. This is the highest-leverage, lowest-effort SEO move visible across all eight sites, and it maps directly onto GSP's existing city set (Eindhoven, Veldhoven, Helmond, Best) crossed with its four disciplines, 4 cities x 4 disciplines = up to 16 targeted landing pages from content GSP mostly already has.

5. **Visible, specific social proof: named clients and cases with an outcome, not testimonial quotes alone.** De ICT Recruiter: 8 client logos + 3 case studies. Iduet: 3 case studies with named CTO-level testimonials. Generalists (Brunel, ALTEN, TMC) lean on scale numbers instead (consultants, countries, offices) rather than named-client cases, that is a size flex a one-person agency cannot copy, but the boutique case-study pattern is directly copyable.

6. **One page, one clear next step, contract-type shown as a filter tag rather than a separate funnel.** YER: detachering/zzp/detavast/direct-to-client shown as tags on the same vacancy list, not as separate "which service do you want" landing pages that force a choice before seeing any job. Reduces friction for a candidate who does not yet know or care which contract type fits.

7. **Content built for hiring-manager search intent, separate from candidate search intent.** De ICT Recruiter's Kennisbank targets "salaris CIO," "CISO aannemen," market-trend queries, deliberately distinct from the job-search content most competitors default to. GSP's blog brief should include at least one hiring-manager-facing content track (e.g. "wat kost een embedded engineer in Brainport," "OT-cybersecurity functieprofiel opstellen") alongside candidate-facing posts.

8. **Vacancy pages keep closed/archived roles visible with named clients, as a trust/proof device rather than deleting them.** De ICT Recruiter shows 75 closed roles alongside 3 open ones, turns "we don't have much live right now" into "look how much we've actually placed." Directly useful for GSP at 27 live vacancies: an archive of filled roles (anonymized if needed, or named where the client consents) demonstrates track record without requiring more live volume.

### Vacancy strategy for GSP

**Current state:** 27 live vacancies across 4 disciplines, Embedded software (8), Mechatronica (7), OT-security (6), Test en verificatie (6), seniority split junior 4 / medior 13 / senior 10, all in Eindhoven/Veldhoven/Helmond/Best (source: `talent-os/backend/data/pool_vacancies.json`, read 2026-09-23).

**Compared to the eight competitors:** the four generalists (TMC ~several hundred across pagination, Brunel low hundreds per aggregator, ALTEN 550+ consultants, YER full HR-solutions catalogue) operate at 10-50x GSP's volume, but across dozens of disciplines nationwide, their per-discipline, per-region density in embedded/mechatronics/OT specifically is not necessarily higher than GSP's. The two boutiques (De ICT Recruiter: 3 open roles live; Iduet: ~6 shown on homepage) run at volumes at or below GSP's 27, confirming that a focused agency legitimately competes on depth, not raw count.

**Recommendation: widen selectively, don't chase generalist volume.** A faceless, one-person, backoffice-partner-supported agency cannot and should not try to match TMC or ALTEN's headcount. The credible move is to widen from four to six-to-eight adjacent disciplines that are a natural extension of the existing embedded/mechatronics/OT positioning, keep every one publicly defensible as "still Brainport high-tech," and grow live-vacancy count through real client orders, not filler.

Disciplines that fit the Brainport region and the current positioning, in priority order:
1. **Cloud/IoT voor embedded systemen** (edge computing, connected devices), direct extension of embedded software, same client base (ASML supply chain, medtech, automotive suppliers).
2. **Functional safety (IEC 61508 / ISO 26262)**, adjacent to OT-security and test/verificatie, high-value niche, few Dutch agencies specialize in it explicitly (none of the eight profiled here lead with it), genuine differentiation opportunity.
3. **Systems engineering**, sits naturally between mechatronica and embedded, common Brainport job title (ASML, Philips, NXP ecosystem), low incremental content cost since it overlaps existing candidate pools.
4. **Industrial IT / OT infrastructure** (distinct from OT-security: network architecture, SCADA/PLC integration), extension of the existing OT-security discipline, same client conversations.
5. **Data engineering voor industrial/manufacturing**, read cautiously: high competition from generalist data-recruitment brands (all eight either have or are building a Data/AI vertical), harder to defend as "Brainport-specific."
6. **SAP/PLM for manufacturing**, plausible but furthest from GSP's core engineering-software identity; only pursue if a specific client asks, not proactively.

**Trade-off, stated plainly:** niche (current 4 disciplines) means every vacancy and blog post reinforces the same search terms and the same small candidate pool, strong SEO relevance, but a hard ceiling on volume and on how many client relationships GSP can serve at once. Broad (adding disciplines 1-4 above) means more addressable clients and more content surface area, but dilutes the "embedded/OT specialist" positioning that differentiates GSP from the generalists (Brunel, ALTEN, YER, TMC) who already win on scale. The eight sites reviewed show both models working; none of the boutiques (De ICT Recruiter, Iduet) went broad, they stayed narrow and leaned on transparent process and pricing instead. GSP's honesty-first, no-hype register is closer to the boutique model, so disciplines 1-3 (natural extensions, same client base, no new positioning claim needed) are safer than 5-6 (would require GSP to credibly claim expertise it does not yet have evidence for).

**Concrete target:** grow from 27 to roughly 40-45 live vacancies over the next two quarters by adding disciplines 1-3 only, sourced exclusively through real client job orders, not by inventing placeholder listings. That keeps the honesty rule intact (every listed vacancy corresponds to an actual client mandate) while giving the site enough breadth per discipline x city combination (aim for at least 2-3 live roles per discipline to support a discipline landing page, and at least 3-4 per city to support a city landing page) to make the city x discipline SEO matrix (pattern 4 above) worth building.

**Ethical sourcing, no scraping of logged-in sources:**
- Public job-board postings from companies GSP already has a relationship with (their own careers pages, published without login).
- Client job orders obtained through the existing GSP-morning-brief / gsp-client-leads Routine pipeline (business development, not scraping).
- Public LinkedIn company job listings (publicly visible, no login required to view) used as leads for outreach, never copied verbatim as GSP's own listing without a signed client mandate.
- Chamber of Commerce (KVK) and public company-registry data to identify Brainport manufacturers likely to need these adjacent disciplines, as lead lists for the sales/intake process, not as a source of fabricated vacancies.

# Deel B: ontwerp van de websites


### Samenvatting (NL)

We hebben acht concurrenten en onze eigen zeven publieke pagina's gescreenshot op 1440px en 390px (Chromium, Playwright) en vergeleken op structuur, vertrouwen en conversie. Computer Futures (STHree) en Randstad Professional hebben de sterkste "harde cijfers" (aantal vacatures, tijd-tot-plaatsing, salaris) zichtbaar in de hero en op de vacaturepagina; De ICT Recruiter en Iduet tonen salaris en een concrete deadline/CTA-paneel op de vacature-detailpagina, iets wat onze `vacature.html` nog niet toont. Onze eigen sterke punten: rustiger, minder opdringerige cookie-flows, een consistent "geen black box"-proces met genummerde stappen (vergelijkbaar met De ICT Recruiter en Iduet, dus geen unieke onderscheiding meer), en een merk zonder gezichten dat overal consistent volgehouden wordt (concurrenten breken dit bijna allemaal met teamfoto's). Zwakke punten bij ons: geen zichtbaar vacature-aantal of "laatst bijgewerkt" nabij de hero, geen salarisrange op de vacature-detailpagina, geen sticky solliciteer-knop op mobiel, en een homepage-hero die tijdens laden een lange lege ruimte laat zien voordat de content verschijnt (zie caveat hieronder). De belangrijkste aanbevelingen, gerangschikt op impact/moeite: (1) salarisrange en "geplaatst op"-datum tonen op de vacature-kaart en -detailpagina (M, gebaseerd op Iduet/De ICT Recruiter/Randstad), (2) een sticky "solliciteer"-balk op mobiele vacature-detail (S, gebaseerd op De ICT Recruiter's zij-paneel en iDuet's inline formulier), (3) een concreet, actueel vacature-aantal + "bijgewerkt vandaag"-indicator naast de hero-zoekbalk (S, gebaseerd op Randstad Professional's "561 vacatures" en Computer Futures' zoekbalk). Geen van de voorstellen introduceert foto's van medewerkers of eigennamen; het merk blijft gezichtloos. Onderaan staat een tekst-wireframe voor de drie hoogst gerangschikte wijzigingen.

Twee bevindingen zijn infrastructureel, geen designfout: onze `vacature.html` en `vacatures.html` laden vacaturedata via `fetch()` naar `api.gsprecruitment.nl` vanuit de browser; die requests dragen niet de `User-Agent: gsp-ops` header die de WAF vereist (zie CLAUDE.md), dus in deze render kregen we "Vacature niet gevonden" / een retry-state in plaats van echte vacaturedata, dit is een renderbeperking van deze sessie, geen bevestigde productiebug, maar wel het waard om te verifiëren met een echte browser tegen productie. Verder liet elke GSP-pagina in de full-page screenshot een opvallende lege ruimte zien met het logo gecentreerd rond scroll-positie ~300–600px, vóór de echte content; dat lijkt een preloader/splash-overlay die niet op tijd verdween voor de full-page capture, vermeld als renderobservatie, niet als bevestigde UX-bug, en de moeite waard om met een live browser te bevestigen.

#### 2. Cross-competitor patterns that build trust and convert

1. **A live, exact vacancy count next to or inside the search action** (Randstad Professional's "561 vacatures" button; De ICT Recruiter's "78 uitgelichte vacatures" headline, though undermined by mostly-closed listings). Signals the pool is real and current.
2. **A real search box in the hero** (keyword + location), not just a "bekijk vacatures" link (Computer Futures, Randstad Professional).
3. **Salary shown on the vacancy card and/or detail page** (Iduet shows a range on both; Darwin and De ICT Recruiter never show it, and feel weaker for it).
4. **A short, numbered "how we work" process near the top or middle of the funnel** (De ICT Recruiter's 4 steps, Iduet's 4-step "onze aanpak", both visually close to our own `.step-num` pattern, this is now table stakes, not a differentiator).
5. **Concrete speed/guarantee numbers repeated near every CTA, not just once on an about page** (Iduet's "25+ jaar / 14–21 dagen / 100% senior" strip appears twice, once under the hero, once again on the local landing page, right by the button).
6. **A clear two-audience split above the fold** (employer vs. candidate), done with cards or a toggle (Computer Futures, ourselves already).
7. **An inline apply flow with no page redirect**, ideally with CV upload right next to the job text (Iduet). Reduces the drop-off between "interested" and "applied."
8. **A service/commercial-model grid that explains contract types plainly** (Darwin's 7-model grid, our own service-ladder, comparable, already built).
9. **Client-logo or aggregate-review social proof placed low-effort but visible** (Randstad Professional's star rating in the footer; Iduet and De ICT Recruiter both run client-logo strips).
10. **Deadline / "geplaatst op" dates on vacancy cards and detail pages** (Iduet shows both a posting-adjacent salary chip and a closing date; De ICT Recruiter shows "geplaatst" in its meta bar).

None of the strong patterns above required a named consultant or founder photo to work, the count, the search box, the salary, the process steps and the guarantee numbers are all facts, not faces, and translate directly to a faceless brand.
